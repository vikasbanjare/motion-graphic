## 0. Method and how far to trust this

- I searched trade press (afaqs, Adgully, ET BrandEquity, LBB, Ads of the World, Campaign Brief, Ad Age), brand newsrooms, studio and crew social posts, and regulatory coverage. Dates were checked against the article timestamp or the post's own date metadata.
- **I did not watch the videos frame by frame.** The search tools return page text, not video. Any "0–3 s / middle / CTA" breakdown below is **reconstructed** from the published description, transcript, crew notes or platform metadata. Each one says whether it is *reported* (stated by the source) or *inferred* (my reconstruction). Runtimes are given only where a source states them. Otherwise they are marked "n/v" (not verified).
- **Finding:** truly *animated / motion-graphics-led* fintech brand films from this window are rare. Most hero work in India and the US is live-action comedy or cinematic film. Motion design shows up in three places: (a) UI-overlay and graphic layers on live footage (Robinhood, ManvsMachine); (b) CG and abstract "money as material" films (Mercury, J.P. Morgan Payments); (c) short social motion spots and AI-generated films (Robinhood HOOD Month, eToro). Indian US-stock launches so far rely on product explainers and creator reels, not crafted animation. **That leaves an open lane for MAPS.**

### 12. Robinhood: "Trade Every Play" (ManvsMachine, 17 Apr 2026)
- **Hook 0–3 s [I]:** extreme slow-motion close-up of an elite sports moment (a "twitch, pivot, dust trail" [R]).
- **Middle [R/I]:** a cinematic deep dive on gameplay "layered with responsive sports IQ and dynamic trading UI": live-action plates with tracked UI cards and odds/price tickers reacting to the play.
- **CTA / end card [I]:** final freeze on the decisive moment, then a Robinhood wordmark and product line (prediction markets).
- **Visual style:** high-frame-rate live action plus motion-designed UI overlay; dark grade; precise kinetic data.
- **Transitions [I]:** speed ramps (real time to near-freeze), UI elements snapping to tracked points, match-cuts on motion.
- **Tagline:** "Trade Every Play."
- **Lesson for MAPS:** UI as a *graphic layer that reacts to a real-world moment*. Swap sports for "the US brands you use every day".

## 3. Performance data and case studies (what is known to work)

Sources are vendor or agency reports, so treat the figures as directional.

- **Meta fintech benchmarks 2026** (AdLibrary, 17 May 2026: https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ad-benchmarks-fintech-2026):
  - Investment apps: CPM $28–45; feed CTR 0.50–0.85%; funded-account CPA $150–400.
  - Reels has the lowest CPM ($12–25), but needs "format-native vertical video that doesn't look like a TV commercial reformatted".
  - **Problem-aware hooks without rate claims outperform by 20–35% on CTR.**
  - Ads that have run 90+ days are a proxy for compliance-safe, high-CTR formulas.
  - "Scroll behaviour on the first 3 seconds governs everything else."
- **Fintech marketing stats 2026** (Web Tonic, 22 Jul 2026: https://www.webtonic.io/blog/fintech-digital-marketing-statistics):
  - Video is 48.6% of fintech Meta creatives, and the **"Bold Statement" hook dominates openings** (Benly).
  - **28-day median creative lifespan**, so a hero film needs a cutdown system.
  - **UGC-style beats polished studio creative by 1.6–2.4x on CPA** (BrighterClick); N26 saw a 65% CAC drop after diversifying into UGC-style.
  - Mobile is 68% of traffic, so vertical-first is mandatory.
- **Animated vs. static** (Cybertize, Jul 2026, citing Meta): animated video ads get about 2x the CTR of static images. This is a secondary source.
- **Organic signal:** the Dhan creator reel reached about 16.1K interactions and 6K likes, against tens to low hundreds on the brand-adjacent posts. Creator-fronted US-stock explainers out-engage brand posts in India, which matches the UGC data.
- **Implication:** the 30 s hero should be crafted motion for brand and TV/CTV. **Plan it to break into 6–10 s vertical modules** and pair it with creator-led explainers for performance.

## 4. India "invest in US stocks": messaging and compliance conventions

### 4.1 Messaging conventions seen in the category (2026)
1. **"Customer → Owner":** you already use Apple, Google, Nvidia, Visa daily; now own a piece (INDmoney, Upstox, Dhan).
2. **Fractional hook:** "from $1" or "₹100 for a slice of a ₹50,000 share" (INDmoney, Upstox, Dhan, Vested; Groww also supports fractional).
3. **Regulated and onshore:** "via GIFT City / IFSC", "IFSCA-regulated", "no US bank account needed" (Dhan, Upstox, Groww).
4. **Cost proof:** zero account or custody fees, a flat percentage brokerage (Dhan 0.25%), "no hidden FX" claims. Viewers in comments actively challenge FX markups.
5. **Diversification / "not every mega theme is at home":** AI, semiconductors, global brands (Dhan, Vested).
6. **Specialist vs. supermarket** positioning (Vested).

### 4.2 Regulatory conventions (verify with MAPS compliance; this is not legal advice)

**SEBI Common Advertisement Code (CAC):** consultation 23 Jun 2026; board approval 24 Sep 2026; **notified 8 Oct 2026**, with a proposed ~6-month transition. Sources: ET CFO 12 Oct 2026 (https://cfo.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy/sebi-notifies-common-ad-code-for-market-intermediaries-allows-celebrity-endorsement-for-brands/134892600); CorpLawUpdates summary of the consultation (https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/sebi-common-advertisement-code-cac-regulated-entities); MediaNama (https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-lowdown-sebi-treat-finfluencers-ai-avatars-celebrities-under-new-ad-code/). It applies to stock brokers, DPs, IAs, RAs, OBPPs, PMS and MFs.

- **Mandatory basic details:** entity name, **SEBI registration number**, entity logo, plus the prescribed disclaimer. The standard broker disclaimer used across the industry is: *"Investments in securities market are subject to market risks, read all the related documents carefully before investing."* Exchange codes require it word for word, with no additions or deletions.
- **Securities shown as examples:** the disclaimer *"The securities are quoted as an example and not as a recommendation"* must sit **in the same frame** as the security, not only at the end. **This directly governs showing Apple, Nvidia or Tesla on screen.**
- **No logos or symbols of SEBI, exchanges or MIIs, "or any other prestigious organisation", unless permitted.** Third-party US company logos are not explicitly listed, but they fall under this "prestigious organisation" catch-all and also raise trademark and implied-endorsement risk.
  - **Industry practice:** competitors name the companies in copy and VO ("Apple, Tesla, Amazon, Microsoft jaise…"), and press coverage lists them.
  - **Safest route:** use **names or tickers in house typography** (AAPL, NVDA) or generic product silhouettes (a phone, a chip, a card), each with the same-frame "example, not recommendation" line. Avoid official logos unless legal clears them.
- **No guaranteed, indicative or assured returns; no past-performance claims** unless done via PaRRVA. **No projections, no testimonials.**
- **Charts and stats** must carry a source and date or period in frame. So a rising-line animation should be **abstract and unlabelled**, or properly sourced.
- **No incentives, vouchers or coupons to drive app downloads or trading.** Do not end on "₹X free" or a sign-up bonus.
- **Dark patterns banned:** no false urgency ("Only today!") and no countdown-to-scarcity framing. A launch-date countdown is fine; fake scarcity is not.
- **Celebrities are now allowed at brand or entity level only**, with **prior approval**, and they cannot endorse a specific product. Under the CAC definition, **"celebrity" includes a virtual character or AI avatar with lifelike human traits** and influencers with more than 5 lakh followers. **An animated human-like mascot or presenter may therefore count as a "celebrity" and trigger prior approval.** Abstract or graphic characters are lower risk.
- **Short-format** placements (push notifications, pop-ups) may hyperlink to full disclaimers. Video spots should carry the text on screen.

**GIFT City / IFSCA route:**
- A US-stock product run through an IFSC entity is regulated by **IFSCA**, not SEBI. The parent broker's ads remain under SEBI CAC.
- The convention (Dhan, Groww, Upstox) is to name the route: "via GIFT City IFSC", "IFSCA-regulated", "aligned with RBI/FEMA".

**LRS and TCS:**
- Both the GIFT City and the direct route **use the US$250,000 per financial year LRS limit**.
- **20% TCS** applies on remittances above ₹10 lakh in a financial year (s.206C(1G)); it is adjustable against tax at ITR filing.
- US stocks are taxed as unlisted foreign securities (24-month LTCG at 12.5%); Schedule FA reporting applies.
- Source: Finnovate, 25 Jun 2026 (https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/gift-city-vs-lrs-global-investing-india).
- Ad convention: a short end-card line such as "Remittances under RBI's LRS (up to US$250,000/FY). TCS may apply. Currency risk applies." Creators add "Currency movement, LRS limits, TCS, taxation… must be clearly understood".

**Ad-platform rules:** on Meta, financial products fall under Special Ad Category / financial-services verification. Platform rules vary by country, so check India-specific Meta and Google financial-services verification before buying media.

### 4.3 Recommended on-screen legal stack for a 30 s film (compliance to confirm)
- **Persistent lower-third** (from about 1 s to the end, small but legible): `MAPS Global Markets [entity] | SEBI Reg. No. XXXX | [IFSC entity / IFSCA Reg. if applicable]`
- **Same-frame** whenever any US company name or ticker appears: `Securities quoted are examples, not recommendations.`
- **End card, about 3 s hold:** `Investments in securities market are subject to market risks; read all related documents carefully before investing. Overseas investments are made under RBI's LRS (up to US$250,000/FY); TCS may apply. Subject to currency risk.`

## 5. Synthesis for the 30 s "MAPS Global Markets — Now invest in US Stocks" film

### Six patterns to adopt
1. **Bold-statement hook within 0–2 s, built on "customer → owner".** Open on the daily-life US brands a viewer touches (phone, search, chip, card) as fast graphic icons, then flip the frame: "You use them every day. Now own them." This is the category's proven hook (INDmoney, Upstox, Dhan copy), and Benly reports bold-statement hooks dominate fintech openings. Use generic product silhouettes plus tickers rather than logos.
2. **One hero object as the continuity device** (J.P. Morgan "Cash Flow", Chime's ball, Mercury's water): a ₹ coin that flips or morphs into a $ coin, or a share "slice", travels through every scene. Object-led match-cuts replace hard cuts, make the 30 s feel like one move, and make 6 s and 10 s cutdowns trivial.
3. **Product truth as a reacting UI layer** (Robinhood × ManvsMachine): one clean UI moment around 12–20 s, such as "₹100 → 0.06 share" with an animated fractional pie filling and a "Buy" tap. Keep it abstract and unlabelled, with no performance line, so it stays out of the CAC chart and stat rules.
4. **A single crisp metaphor with an "old → stuck → free" turn** (Wealthsimple, Revolut, IG Markets, Pop): global investing used to mean a foreign account, paperwork and wire transfers. That clutter collapses and the coin flows through a "GIFT City / IFSC" gateway. Name the route as the trust proof rather than hide it.
5. **The logo device as transition grammar, plus a launch-system end card** (eToro brackets; Robinhood HOOD Month): make the MAPS mark a window or wipe shape that frames each beat. End about 26–30 s on "Now invest in US Stocks" with the lockup, the app CTA and the legal stack held for at least 3 s. The launch or space language lets you serialise teasers ("T-minus") without fake urgency.
6. **Build for the system, not just the spot.** Make it vertical-first (68% mobile; Reels is the cheapest CPM), sound-off legible with kinetic type, and modular into 3–4 single-idea cutdowns (CRED Foundation one-idea-per-film; 28-day creative half-life). Run a creator-explainer track alongside, since UGC beats studio creative 1.6–2.4x on CPA.

**Suggested 30 s beat sheet:**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Hook: icons of everyday US products snap in. "You use them every day." |
| 2–5 s | Twist: "But do you own them?" The ₹ coin enters. |
| 5–12 s | Old way: paperwork and foreign-account clutter piles up, then collapses (match-cut on the coin). |
| 12–20 s | New way: the coin flows through the MAPS bracket and GIFT City gate and flips to $. UI beat: "Start from $1 / ₹100", fractional slice fills. Tickers appear with the same-frame "examples, not recommendations" line. |
| 20–26 s | Payoff: the coin lands as a slice of ownership. "Now invest in US Stocks." |
| 26–30 s | End card: MAPS Global Markets lockup + app CTA + full disclaimer (≥ 3 s hold). |

### Three things to avoid
1. **Official US company logos, return figures and "number goes up" charts.** The CAC bans prestigious-organisation logos without permission, implied past performance, projections, and unsourced charts. It also requires the "example, not recommendation" line in the *same frame* as any named stock. Use generic iconography, tickers and abstract motion instead.
2. **Inducements and fake urgency in the CTA.** No "free stock", vouchers or download bonuses (CAC prohibition; Robinhood's US "free stock" playbook does not transfer to India). No scarcity countdowns or dark-pattern "only today" framing.
3. **A human-like animated mascot, AI avatar or finfluencer as presenter, and anything that looks generic.** Under the CAC, virtual characters and influencers with more than 5 lakh followers count as "celebrities", which triggers prior approval and bans product-specific endorsement. Fully AI-generated looks (eToro) also risk reading as a gimmick. Separately, do not ship a me-too "global brands now in your reach" montage: Groww, Upstox, Dhan, INDmoney and Vested all launched or ran US-stock comms in Jun–Oct 2026, so craft and one ownable metaphor are the differentiator.
