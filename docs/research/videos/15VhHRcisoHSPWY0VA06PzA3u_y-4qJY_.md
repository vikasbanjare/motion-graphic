## Summary

This is a 54 s brand plus product film for Wix's AI site builder. The film is built as a **bookended typographic sentence** ("Build a website you ♥ love… with WIX" → "Easy to ♥ love" → WIX). Between the bookends it runs a **real-UI demo montage** in three acts:
1. A conversational AI prompt generates sites for three use cases, shown as a "slot machine": skincare, fashion, basketball.
2. A run of AI editing features: theme, image creator, object eraser, text tone, section generation.
3. A business-features montage: store, blog/membership, bookings, classes.

It climaxes on a 3D fanned deck of all the generated sites. Then the deck collapses back into the inline "heart" of the tagline and resolves to the logo.

What makes it look expensive:
- **Strict colour semantics.** Cream and navy mean the brand voice. Cyan, blue and iridescent mean "AI is working". The generated sites carry their own bold editorial palettes.
- **Anchored continuity.** A prompt bar stays locked for 3.8 s across 6 shots. An ice-bottle asset stays put across 4 background swaps. The same product bottle recurs across a theme swap, re-laid-out rather than pixel-locked.
- **Believable cursor and typing physics.** Ballistic cursor moves with long ease-out tails. Typing speed changes with the shot scale.
- **Moves that decelerate into a dwell, then accelerate out into the cut.**

Measured edit: **34 shots (corrected, see Verification), ASL 1.58 s, median 1.50 s.** Two fast clusters average 0.64 s and 0.83 s per shot. The music is about 112 BPM, integrated loudness −15.9 LUFS, music only with vocal-chop textures [inferred]. Cuts are **not beat-locked**: 7 of the 33 corrected cuts fall within ±2 f of an onset, against 5.8 expected by chance. Instead, the music marks the big section changes: the drop at 4.5 s lands 8 f after the prompt cut, the suck-out at 12.2 s lands on the send click (8 f before the BASELINE cut), and the re-entry at 33.4 s lands 2 f after the wall cut.

## Creative Direction

- **Concept:** "Love, made easy". The heart stands for the user's emotion and also works as a UI object: it becomes an inline image slot, a keychain chip, and finally the logo field. The AI is shown as a calm, conversational collaborator (a chat prompt), never as a robot or a sci-fi effect.
- **Visual language:** two worlds, deliberately contrasted.
  1. **Brand frame:** warm off-white #F9F5F2 or deep navy #02003E. The type is a small, regular-weight geometric grotesk. Only one saturated accent appears: the red 3D heart.
  2. **Product world:** real Wix UI (white pills, 1 px hairlines, blue #116DFF-ish primary [inferred]) laid over full-bleed, high-fashion editorial photography. The generated sites use loud display type (BASELINE, DESIGNED TO BE SEEN, SUMMER).
- **AI visual signature** (used consistently 9 times):
  - pastel iridescent gradient fields
  - cyan-to-indigo gradient text for AI-written words
  - a **false-colour "thermal" render** (cyan/violet duotone) that "develops" into the real photo
  - pixel-mosaic de-pixelation
  - a soft violet halo bloom behind a selected element
- **Personality:** confident, warm, fashion-literate, a little playful (emoji heart, inline image chips), never jokey.
- **Positioning mix:** about 40% premium/editorial, 30% playful, 20% technical (real UI, real cursor), 10% cinematic (3D deck, world-integrated page).
- **Consistency:** very high.
  - A single UI kit and a single cursor.
  - The cyan "AI" colour appears only when AI acts.
  - Every generated site is art-directed to a distinct sub-brand (Baseline, Soleu, Bowy, the basketball club), and each recurs later: Soleu in the theme, image and blog demos; Bowy in the text, section and commerce demos; the basketball club in bookings and classes. This makes the montage feel like three coherent customer stories rather than random screens.

### WHY notes for the important shots

- **#1 → #2 (scale-contrast type cut).** The same words jump from 49% FH to ~6.6% FH in one cut. The jump reads as "zooming out to see the whole idea". The macro shot gives a physical hook in 0.6 s. The small line then signals editorial restraint, which is what premium brands do. A long, large headline would read as an ad; a small, quiet sentence reads as confidence.
- **#2 (inline chips and heart).** Swapping words for photos and objects inside a sentence explains "website = your content" without a VO. The 1.0 s static hold before anything moves makes the line legible first. Motion then adds information rather than noise.
- **#2 → #3 (sparkle → prompt morph).** The brand line turns into the product interface. There is no cut to a "screen recording", so the viewer never leaves the brand world. This is the most transferable idea in the film: **the CTA icon becomes the UI**.
- **#4 (ECU typing with caret-follow camera).** A ~3.4× cut-in makes the user's intent ("skincare brand") the hero. Slowing typing to ~7–8 chars/s at ECU and accelerating the camera to keep the caret in the right third keeps the text readable on a phone.
- **#5–#9 (locked prompt bar, swapping worlds).** One UI object holds still while three industries flash behind it. The continuity of the bar lets the background cut every 0.23–1.27 s without disorientation. The thermal → colour switch is an honest "generation" metaphor without fake progress bars.
- **#9 → #10 (send click + audio suck-out + colour match).** Silence (−35 dB) at the click turns the page build into an event. Orange-to-orange makes the cut feel like one surface materialising. The header resolving from cyan to the brand colour shows the AI handing over to the design.
- **#11 (carousel dwell-and-whip).** Decel → dwell → accel gives the eye a beat to read the cards, then the whip creates energy. The music re-enters (14.9–15.0 s) exactly as the whip starts accelerating; the whip peaks at 15.27 s and lands on Soleu by ~15.87 s.
- **#12 → #13 (theme regen with the product carried over).** The same pump bottle survives the cut, moved to the centre and enlarged, so the viewer reads the new frame as the same brand restyled: palette, photo and layout all change around a recurring hero product. It is the film's "before/after" grammar, though weaker than a pixel-locked anchor (compare the ice bottle, #18–#22).
- **#14 (page floating in its own world).** Pulling out to reveal the website as a window over the same beach photo turns a flat UI into a place. It is cinematic depth using only 2D layers and a shadow.
- **#18–#22 (ice-bottle anchor).** A single 3D asset carried across 4 layouts in 2.5 s shows design range. The editor selection box keeps it honest: this is a user placing an asset, not VFX.
- **#24 (AI tone rewrite).** The letters change in the same cyan gradient used everywhere for AI. That colour semantic is now learned, so no caption is needed.

## Motion Language observed

- **Two speed families.**
  - Brand frame: slow and precise (holds of 1.0–1.7 s, single-element moves of 3–6 f).
  - Product world: fast but physical (12 f scrolls, 6 f morphs, 1–2 f pops).
- **Ease-out dominates entrances.** Word rises, chip pops, scroll landings and the whip landing all ease out with an exponential tail (per-frame displacement halves about every 2 f).
- **Decel → dwell → accel moves.** This shape shows up twice: the carousel (14.13–15.27 s) and the deck (48.27–49.63 s). The panel push (28.9–29.47 s) is a pure acceleration. The deck and the panel push cut at peak speed, which hides the cut and keeps momentum. The carousel does not: it lands with a ~16 f ease-out, holds, and only then a 3 f zoom accelerates into the cut-in.
- **Stagger:** 2–3 f for UI chips, 4 f (133 ms) for floating cards, 5–7 f for bigger objects (calendar, sticker). Order is always reading order or importance order.
- **"AI acts" grammar:** every AI action is a gradient sweep, or a thermal/pixel state that switches or dissolves to colour (switch in 1 f plus a 6–12 f clean-up, or a 15–20 f dissolve), then the final state. Non-AI actions (cursor, scroll, popups) have no colour effect.
- **Microinteractions:**
  - sparkle twinkle on hover
  - button fills cyan in 1 f on press
  - selection outline plus contextual toolbar on click
  - slider thumb drag
  - date highlight
- **No overshoot or elastic bounce on UI.** Any overshoot is subtle and limited to chip and heart pops [inferred]. This restraint reads premium.
- **Anchoring:** something usually stays still while the context changes. Examples: the prompt bar, the ice asset, the inline sentence position. The theme-swap bottle is a looser version: it recurs but is moved and enlarged.
- **3D used sparingly:** the spinning heart, the ice object, the carousel and deck perspectives. Everything else is 2D, so the 3D moments read as special.

- **Beat sync:**

## Style Category

**UI-Focused AI Product Demo inside an Editorial Brand Frame** ("Brand-Bookended Product Montage"). It is a hybrid of Minimal Premium SaaS (brand frames), UI-Focused Demo (real product interactions) and Editorial Motion (fashion-magazine content, large display type). A discovered sub-style is worth naming: **"Generative Slot-Machine"**, where one fixed UI element stays put while use cases rotate behind it.

## Transferable Rules

1. **Bookend with one sentence.** Open on a ≤7-word promise; close on a ≤3-word echo plus the logo. Brand-frame type cap height ~6.5% FH (~70 px @1080), regular weight, centred, ≥1.0 s still before motion.
2. **Hook with a scale contrast.** 0.5–0.7 s of macro type at 45–50% FH, word-by-word (new word every 7 f, 6 f ease-out rise of 8–15% FH, jump-reframing on each new word), then hard cut to the same words at ~6.6% FH.
3. **Let the CTA icon become the UI.** Animate the brand-line icon into the product input over 6 f, fading context over the same 6 f, then cut. Never cut from the brand world to a cold screen recording.
4. **One colour = AI.** Reserve a single gradient family (cyan → indigo, or iridescent pastel) for "AI is acting". Use it as an 8–15 f transitional state that always settles to final colours. Never leave it on resting UI.
5. **Show generation as a develop, not a progress bar.** Duotone/thermal or pixel-mosaic for 7–15 f, then either switch to the true image in 1 f and clear any residual tint over 6–12 f (montage pace), or dissolve over 15–20 f (a single hero image).
6. **Anchor one element across rapid cuts.** When the ASL drops below 0.8 s, lock a UI element or product to identical screen coordinates across the shots. Swap only the context.
7. **Slot-machine montage.** 3 use cases at about 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.6 s each (lengthening), then a payoff on the last one.
8. **Silence before a reveal.** Drop the music by ≥15 dB on the decisive click and hold the gap 0.3–0.5 s, cutting to the result ~8 f after the click. Build the result in 12 f with a staggered order: container → headline → badge → nav → body (2–4 f apart).
9. **Decel → dwell → accel bridges.** For carousels and decks: exponential decel over 11–14 f, a dwell of ~6–7 f at <15% of peak speed, then accelerate over 13–21 f. Either cut at peak speed (deck), or land the move with a ~16 f ease-out on the next hero and hold before cutting in (carousel).
10. **Cursor physics.** Native-size arrow (~2% FH). 15–20 f moves with peak speed on frame 3–4 (~80 px/f @1080) and a 10–12 f ease-out tail. Hover 0.3–1.0 s before a click. Confirm the click with a 1 f state change.
11. **Typing speeds.** Wide shots 18–20 chars/s; ECU 7–8 chars/s; headline type-on up to ~50 chars/s with a gradient leading edge. Keep the caret in the right third with an ease-in camera follow.
12. **Stagger ladder.** Chips 2–3 f; cards 4 f (133 ms), each popping whole in 1 f; large objects 5–7 f. Use reading order.
13. **Cut-ins over zoom animations.** Use ~3–3.5× scale cut-ins for detail (measured 3.4× at 6.57 s) and pull-out cuts (to 85%, 55% or 44%) for context. Reserve animated pushes for 1–2 moments: a 3 f accelerating ~1.5× zoom that throws into a cut-in, or 130% in 0.5 s only into an action.
14. **Before/after with a recurring product.** For theme or style changes, keep the hero object on screen across the hard cut after a 12 f shimmer. This film moves and enlarges the bottle; pixel-locking it, as the film does with the ice asset, would make the before/after even clearer [recommendation].
15. **Climax = collection.** End the demo with all generated outputs in one 3D object (deck or wall), then fold it back into the opening motif in 6 f.
16. **Logo:** cap height ~12% FH, centred, revealed by a frame-filling motif cut plus an exponential colour dissolve (strong colour gone in 8 f, settled in ~25 f), held ~1.7 s after it settles, audio fade over ~2.2 s.
17. **Music at section level.** Place the drop within 0–8 f of the first product UI. Pull back under dense visual montages; re-enter within ±2 f of the next big section cut. Don't force every cut onto a beat in UI-driven films.

## What to Avoid

- Changing on-screen copy between a wide shot and its cut-in (the "make" vs "create" error). Lock strings across a scene.
- UI micro-copy under ~2.5% FH when it carries meaning. Either enlarge it, cut in, or accept it as texture.
- Unexplained asset montages (the ice bottle). Give each montage one readable claim or a UI label.
- Holding AI effect colours or duotone longer than ~15 f, which reads as a glitch.
- Delivering at a mismatched frame rate (a 25 fps master into a 30 fps container) or under ~2 Mb/s for 720p gradients. Banding and judder cheapen premium work.
- Bouncy or elastic UI pops. This film's credibility comes from damped, single-direction ease-outs.
- Adding whooshes to every whip. The film relies on the music arrangement plus a few click accents.
