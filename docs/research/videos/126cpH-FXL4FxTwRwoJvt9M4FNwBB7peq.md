## Summary

A 17.97 s (the TikTok cuts out at 17.97 s, so the ad may continue [inferred]) **9:16 consumer-app launch ad for Chowdeck**, a Nigerian food and grocery delivery app. It is built as a **playful flat-illustration plus photo-cutout collage with a UI-demo middle act**. The story follows a single order: a Pidgin hook question "You don chow?" ("Have you eaten?"), then "been busy?" (live-action hands on a phone already showing the Chowdeck app), then "order in seconds" with a cel-style starburst. Next comes a mosaic of grocery tiles in which flat silhouettes swap into real product photos that then fall with gravity. Then a UI order card swings in, a cursor clicks "Order now", and the card morphs into a notification over a map whose route draws while the status cycles ("Order ready", then "Rider on his way to you", then "Order delivered"). The payoff is an illustrated "Order Delivered" hand-over. A camera tilt down through clouds lands on the Chowdeck wordmark lockup over a green landscape with a tiny rider.

What makes it work: a **strict 6-colour brand palette**, **teal bookends** (the first and last shots share the brand teal), **shape-matched flat-to-real swaps**, **shared-element UI morphing** (card to notification), **on-twos tactility**, and a **local-language voice**. It is not "premium SaaS" in the Apple/Linear sense. It is **crafted, playful, brand-forward commerce motion**, and its UI-demo and continuity tricks transfer directly to SaaS.

## Creative Direction

- **Concept:** "From hunger to doorstep in one breath." Every shot advances the life of a single order: question, need, promise, choice, order, tracking, delivery, brand.
- **Visual language:** flat vector illustration (2D, no gradients, cel-style starburst, organic blobs, long shadow) collaged with **real photo cut-outs** (banana, milk jug, melon, live-action hands and phone) and **clean light-UI cards** (cream cards, teal primary button, orange secondary). The UI also lives in the flat world: no device frame, cards float on flat colour.
- **Personality:** cheeky, warm, local and energetic. Pidgin copy, lowercase display type, things popping and falling, a stop-motion feel from animating on 2s.
- **Tone:** friendly reassurance ("we've got you"), not aspiration.
- **Positioning mix:** playful about 60%, crafted/premium about 20% (palette discipline, continuity), technical/UI about 15%, cinematic about 5% (one camera tilt through a cloud layer).
- **Consistency:** very high on palette and type family. Medium on rendering style: three idioms (flat illustration, photo cut-outs, light UI) are mixed deliberately, and the palette keeps them unified. The background colour is used as the chapter marker: teal for the brand and promise, cream/yellow for the product and order chapter, light grey for the map, then back to teal for the brand.

### WHY notes for the important shots

- **#1 Hook.** The Pidgin question does the targeting: a Lagos viewer feels addressed by name in 3 words. The pop-on "chow?" with background blobs appearing in the same frame turns a static card into an event at 0.73 s. Cost: the first 20 f are static (then only a small pill nudge before the pop), which only works because the TikTok POV caption carries the hook for the outer platform.
- **#2 Matte reveal.** Opening the photo as a **flat yellow silhouette** keeps the graphic language going for 8 f, so live action enters the flat world without a style shock. The silhouette is also in the type colour, so it reads as "the type bled into the photo". The phone on screen already shows the app (product placement inside the problem).
- **#3 Burst.** The single loudest visual in the ad sits on the core claim "seconds". The rays last only about 0.7 s, then the icon "falls off". This undercuts the bombast with humour and also clears the stage for the next shot.
- **#4-6 Mosaic with flat-to-real swaps.** The flat icons act as a **promise** and the real photos as **proof** ("these are real groceries"). Same silhouette, same position, swapped instantly on one pose change (no dissolve), so the eye stays locked. The gravity fall after each swap gives physical weight and motivates the next beat. The two-stage pull-out (an expo-out first stage, then an ease-in-out second stage, about 4.1x in total over 1.0 s) makes the grid feel like it "opens up".
- **#7 UI card.** The tilted entry plus fast settle reads as "a card was tossed onto the table": casual, human, on-brand. The cursor's 10-f dwell before the press is the anticipation that makes the click land. The click coincides with an audio onset (0 f offset) after a 0.5 s audio dip.
- **#8 Shared element.** The order card becomes the notification and **stays on screen through the scene change**. The viewer tracks one object while the world changes behind it (rack focus to map), the clearest continuity device in the ad. The status cycling inside one container shows time passing without new shots.
- **#9-10 Payoff then tilt.** The hand-over is the emotional end of the order story. The tilt down through the clouds turns "delivered" into "brand", and returning to the opening teal closes the loop.

## Frame-by-Frame Studies

All zoom sheets were made with `frames.py` on the crop (30 fps unless noted).

1. **Hook pop and sparkle (0.00-1.60 s, 48 f).** Pill static f0-f19 (667 ms; the slow creep in that window matches the monitor edge, so it is phone drift). f20-f22 pill shifts down about 1.5-2%H (about 3 f, 100 ms). f22 (0.733 s): "chow?" plus the 45° long shadow appear, and the faint blob shapes swell, **in a single frame** (diff spike 8.5-10.5 vs a 1-2.5 baseline). There is no scale, slide or overshoot, so the "pop" is a step change. The sparkle (4-point star, about 3%H) jumps position every 2-3 f along the word outline from the "?" to the "c" over about 22 f, a "twinkle travel". Blobs re-shape each 12 fps pose ("boil"). *Why it works:* step changes on twos read as hand-made and punchy. The boil keeps a held title alive without moving it.
2. **Yellow matte to photo plus typewriter (1.50-2.70 s, 36 f).** f2 (1.567): 1-f mix of the yellow silhouette over "chow?". 1.60-1.83 (8 f, 267 ms): the background resolves from heavy blur (about 8-10% frame-width radius) to sharp, ease-out, while the subject stays a flat yellow matte. 1.867: the matte is removed in 1 f, revealing the colour photo. Typewriter: "b"/"b" at 1.733, then about 1 glyph per 12 fps pose, "?" at 2.067. That is about 5 glyphs in 10 f (333 ms, about 15 chars/s). Both lines type simultaneously. *Why:* a two-stage reveal (background first, subject second) gives the eye an order of attention. Typing a question mimics chat and messaging.
3. **Vertical push to "order in seconds" (2.50-3.47 s, 30 f).** Teal boundary position (fraction of height from the top, measured on both edge strips of the crop): 2.533 = 0.89-0.91, 2.600 = 0.76-0.81, 2.667 = one blurred/blended frame, 2.700-2.733 = 0.49-0.50, 2.767-2.800 = 0.14-0.16, 2.833 = 0.06-0.13, 2.867 = 0.04-0.07, 2.900 = 0-0.03, 2.933 = 0. That is 1.0 frame height in about 11-12 f (2.533-2.90, about 370 ms). Per-pose deltas of about 11%, 29%, 35%, 6%, 4%, 3% show a **steep ease-in-out with a fast-decay tail** (expo-in-out-like), with peak speed about 4.2 frame-heights/s. Directional vertical blur on both layers is about 10%H at peak. "seconds" blurs in at 3.067 and is sharp at 3.20 (4 f, no measurable scale change). *Why:* the push keeps the direction of reading (down to up = forward), and the blur hides the 12 fps stepping.
4. **Starburst (3.80-5.30 s, 45 f).** 3.90: star plus first rays. 4.033: rays reach the frame edges (4 f, 133 ms, expo-out). 4.03-4.43: rays rotate and change width every pose (12 f). 4.47-4.57: rays thin to hairlines (3 f). 4.60: rays gone, star remains to 4.73. 4.77: star off. Icon tilts onto the word at 4.87-4.93, rests to 5.00, then drops 5.03-5.10 with accelerating per-pose displacement (about 6%, 7%, 16%H): **gravity ease-in**. Dissolve to the jug at 5.167 (1 f). Total 37 f (1.23 s). *Why:* an explosive in (4 f), busy middle, and quick out (3 f) give maximum punch for minimum screen time. The comedic fall exit avoids an abrupt cut.
5. **Jug ECU to mosaic (5.10-6.40 s, 39 f).** Jug height (%H, measured per frame from the orange mask, cross-checked by width): 5.20 = 69, 5.27 = 59, 5.37 = 50, 5.43 = 46, 5.53 = 44, 5.70 = 43, 5.77 = 41, then the second stage: 5.87 = 36, 5.93 = 28, 6.03 = 20, 6.10 = 18, 6.20 = 17, held at 17 through 6.50. That is a **two-stage pull-out**, about 4.1x total over 1.0 s (5.20-6.20). Stage 1 is expo-out (about 40% of its change in the first pose, about 90% within 8 f). Stage 2 is ease-in-out (per-pose steps of 5, 8, 8, 2 and 1%H), not a 2-f snap. The banana swings ±10° per pose while sliding in from the top edge. Tile colour animates white to peach at 5.50 (1 pose). *Why:* starting on an ECU of one icon and pulling back to reveal a system ("one item, now everything") is a classic scale-reveal, and the two-stage move (with a near-hold of about 0.25 s between stages) gives it rhythm.
6. **Flat-to-real swaps plus falls (6.40-9.40 s at 15 fps; swaps re-checked frame by frame).** Banana swap: flat pose from 6.70, real banana from the next pose at 6.767 (the real banana appears with a soft drop shadow at the same silhouette and angle). Jug swap: flat through 7.633, real milk jug at 7.70. Melon swap: blue oval through 8.40, melon at 8.433, on the same pose as a small camera jump. All three are **instant swaps on one pose change**; the single blended frames at 6.733, 7.667 and 8.367 are capture ghosts of the same kind seen at non-swap pose changes (e.g. 6.667, 7.233, 7.333), not a designed dissolve. Banana fall about 7.27-7.73: rotates about 70-90° clockwise while dropping about 50%H, with accelerating displacement. The milk jug also drops out 8.27-8.47. Camera: push-in about 1.3x plus pan right from about 6.53 to 8.4, roughly linear. *Why:* a shape-matched swap is a cheap, robust "morph" that never warps geometry. It works as a model for AI generation too (a hard switch between two stable states rather than a generated morph).
7. **UI card swing-in (9.20-10.40 s, 36 f).** Card top-edge angle (fitted on the middle of the card's top edge): about +15° at 9.267-9.333 (one pose held 3 f), +1.6° at 9.367, +0.6° at 9.40, then an **overshoot to about -3.5 to -4°** at 9.433-9.60, -1.2 to -1.6° at 9.70-9.87 and about 0° by 10.00. ## Motion Language observed

- **Animated on twos (12 fps) over a 24 fps base** [inferred]. Every graphic pose holds 2-3 capture frames. This gives a tactile, hand-made, stop-motion feel that suits flat illustration and hides cheap tweening. Live action stays on ones.
- **Pops and snaps over tweens.** Most entrances take 0-4 f (0-133 ms): pop-on title, 4-f burst, 3-f card swing, instant flat-to-real swaps. Settles are short (16-22 f at most). The exceptions are the soft headline fade (about 15 f) and the route draw.
- **Two-stage pull-out**: an expo-out first stage (about 40% of its scale change in the first pose, about 90% in 8 f), a near-hold, then an ease-in-out second stage. About 4.1x in total.
- **Gravity physics as a motif:** clock icon, banana, jug and melon all fall with ease-in acceleration and rotation, and the melon even falls across a cut. It ties the grocery theme to "things dropping into your basket".

- **Typewriter** for the question (about 15 chars/s). **Blur-in** for the claim word (4 f). **Staggered opacity fade** for the payoff (about 15 f per line, 3-f stagger).

## Style Category

**Playful Illustrated Collage plus UI Demo (consumer-app launch).** This is a discovered category that sits between "Fast Startup Launch" and "Editorial Motion": flat vector illustration plus photo cut-outs, animated on twos, bold lowercase colloquial type, colour-blocked chapters, and a UI demo staged as a story of a single transaction. Duration 15-20 s, 9:16, music-led groove at 95-110 BPM, no VO. Products: consumer apps (delivery, fintech, social), and SaaS brands that want warmth over sleekness.

## Transferable Rules

1. **Dialect hook:** open with a ≤ 3-word question in the audience's own idiom. Hero word ascender 12-13%H, about 85% width, a small setup word above at 1/3 size. Have it on screen by frame 0 and make the first change happen ≤ 0.5 s in (this ad waits 0.73 s, which is too long).
2. **Single-transaction story:** structure a 15-20 s app ad as Hook 1.6 s, Problem 0.9 s, Promise 2.6 s, Range/Benefit 4 s, UI action 1.8 s, Progress/Tracking 2.5 s, Payoff 1.6 s, Transition 1.0 s, Lockup ≥ 2.5 s with CTA.
3. **Chapter colour coding:** one anchor brand colour opens and closes the film. Change the background colour per chapter (≤ 4 backgrounds per 18 s).
4. **Shape-matched flat-to-real swap:** same silhouette, position and angle, swapped instantly on one pose change (this ad uses no dissolve), then a physical reaction (fall/rotate, 6-14 f, ease-in) within about 0.6 s. Use it instead of AI morphing.
5. **Two-stage pull-out reveal:** from an ECU of one icon to the full system, about 4x in total over about 1 s. Stage 1 is expo-out (about 40% of its change in the first 2 f, about 90% within 8 f). Hold nearly still for about 0.25 s, then run stage 2 ease-in-out over about 13 f.
6. **Tossed-card UI entrance:** start rotated about 15°, get about 90% of the way upright on the next pose (3 f), overshoot about 4° the other way for about 5 f, and settle by 16-22 f. Use an offset solid backing card (4-6% offset) for depth in flat styles.
7. **Cursor grammar:** 8-f ease-out approach, **10-f dwell**, 5-f press effect, then a 10-f hold before the consequence. Put an audio accent on the press frame (0 f) after a ≥ 0.3 s audio dip.
8. **Shared-element morph** between UI scenes: drop the card's offset backing, collapse it vertically toward its bottom edge and narrow it into the target pill over about 7 f (3 poses), and rack-focus the background over 11-12 f. (Adding a +5% anticipation pop first is optional; this ad does not do it.) The anchor element never leaves the screen.
9. **Status cycling in one container:** swap text only (fade-out, 1-3 f blank, fade-in; 5-7 f) and hold each status ≥ 1.0 s (≤ 5 words). This ad gives that hold only to "Rider on his way to you" and shows "Order delivered" for 2 f, which is too short. Draw progress (route or bar) across most of the shot (about 1.7 s here, leg by leg, easing into corners) so it reaches the destination just before the final status. Bring the map in tilted 10-15° and ease it upright over about 1 s, then a slow linear pan (about 8%H per second).
10. **Vertical push transition:** 11 f, expo-in-out, directional blur about 10%H at peak on both layers. Reading direction bottom-to-top means "forward".
11. **Seamless tilt to the lockup:** 30 f S-curve through a horizontal layer (clouds, horizon, UI edge), then about a 10-f settle. Fade the logo in over the last 8 f of the fast move while it rides up with the camera (about 23%H here, 65 to 42%H) to its rest position.
12. **Typewriter questions** at about 15 chars/s. **Blur-in** claim words in 4 f. **Payoff headlines** fade up over about 15 f per line with a 3-f line stagger.
13. **On-twos (12 fps) only for illustration/collage layers.** Keep live action, UI scrolling and camera moves on ones in SaaS work [inferred]: stepped UI motion reads as lag.
14. **Hold budget:** each display phrase gets ≥ 0.7 s fully still after its reveal. The payoff gets ≥ 1.0 s and the lockup ≥ 1.5 s, plus one tiny ambient motion (e.g. the rider) so the end frame is not dead.
15. **Event density:** about 1 visual event per 0.7 s overall, with 2-4 s burst/breathe cycles. Slow down (≥ 1.8 s shots) whenever UI must be read.

## What to Avoid

- Static opening frames (> 0.5 s) before the first change.
- Decorative bursts or rays that cross the key claim word. Mask rays behind the type.
- Micro-UI text below about 2.5%H when the UI is the subject. Enlarge the card or crop into the relevant row.
- Ending without a CTA or tagline repeat. The brand lockup alone does not convert.
- Mixing more than 3 rendering idioms in one frame (the mosaic briefly has flat, photo, wordmark and blobs).
- Pop-ons for every element. Reserve 1-f pops for the hook and use short tweens for supporting text.
- Dated flourishes (45° long shadows) in a modern SaaS context.
- Treating screen-capture ghosting as a design "echo" effect when you reverse-engineer from screen recordings.
- Assuming music sync: here none of the 4 hard cuts land within ±2 f of an onset (the nearest is 7 f away). If the brief is music-led, cut on beats deliberately (≥ 60% of cuts within ±2 f).
