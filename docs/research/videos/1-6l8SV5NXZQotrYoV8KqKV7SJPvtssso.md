# Teardown: kivi (heykivi.ai): "Everything. Powered by Your Voice." voice-AI launch film

- File: `refs/1-6l8SV5NXZQotrYoV8KqKV7SJPvtssso.mp4`. Runs 77.78 s at 30 fps, 1138x640 (16:9). Audio is music plus in-world dictation voice. Integrated loudness is -10.7 LUFS.
- Method: I viewed every timeline, overview, hook, shots and transitions sheet. I then made 26 frame-by-frame zoom sheets (30/20/15/12/8 fps, some cropped to the text band), sampled pixel colours and measured text bounding boxes on full-resolution frames. I also rendered three spectrograms (0-26 s, 26-52 s and 52-78 s) and fitted onsets to a beat grid. Numbers are measured unless marked [inferred]. Pixel distances are native 1138x640 unless a 1080p equivalent is given.

## 1. Summary

kivi is a voice-dictation and voice-command AI layer for the desktop. You speak and it writes clean text into Gmail, WhatsApp, a code editor or Excel. The film is a **light, airy, "breathing" minimal-premium SaaS launch** built from three repeating materials:

1. **White and mint aurora type cards.** Large regular-weight sans in sentence case. Each line is built word by word like live transcription, and the second phrase is set in sage green.
2. **Frosted-glass prompt cards with live waveforms.** These sit over heavily blurred, painterly "life" scenes (a desk, an Indian courtyard, a coder at night, an office). Several scenes start in **black and white and bloom into colour as the voice starts**.
3. **Result UI.** An email, a translation, a code card and a spreadsheet each populate in cascades.

Four app demos run on one fixed "say it, then see it" template: title card, then voice card, then output UI. They are bracketed by a 6 s rhetorical hook ("Still typing? Just think out loud. Meet kivi") and a 6.7 s end ("Everything. Powered by Your Voice." → wordmark → hummingbird mark → URL). The two signatures are an **exponential scale "punch" into the cut** (used four times) and **lerp-style auto-recentring kinetic type** (used six times). In the back half, 4 of 6 hard cuts land within ±1.2 frames of a detected music onset, and 5 of 6 land within ±1.1 f of the 127 BPM eighth-note grid.

## 2. Creative Direction

| Aspect | Observation |
|---|---|
| Concept | "Stop typing and think out loud." Voice is a natural, human input. The AI quietly turns messy speech into polished output in any app or language. |
| Visual language | Soft white canvas with drifting mint, lime and aqua aurora blobs (the "voice field"). Frosted-glass UI on blurred illustrated life scenes. Restrained real-app icons (Gmail, WhatsApp, Excel). One serif wordmark against an all-sans system. |
| Personality | Calm, friendly, humane, optimistic. Leans "wellness tech" rather than "power tool". The Indian locations (Koramangala, Kannada, Grandma) give it warmth and local specificity. |
| Tone | Conversational second person ("Your message", "Your voice", "Your spreadsheet"). Questions and imperatives ("Still typing?", "Just speak"). |
| Positioning | About 50% premium-minimal, 25% cinematic (the B&W-to-colour blooms, DOF scenes, light blooms), 20% technical (code card, spreadsheet) and 5% playful (the pill shockwave). |
| Consistency | Very high. The same type system, the same two-tone line build, the same glass card plus waveform, the same aurora and the same punch-in device run across all 33 shots. The weakest point is the style of the background illustrations, which changes from scene to scene (see Weaknesses). |
| Art-direction rule | Light colour means kivi or the "solution": white, mint and aurora. Grey, desaturated or dark colour means the human "problem" world before kivi listens. Colour floods in when the voice starts. |

### WHY notes on the important shots

- **Shot 1 (hook).** The line literally types itself. Word appends plus auto-recentring imitate a live transcription cursor, so the medium (voice to text) is the message from frame 3. A question as the first word engages the viewer with no logo or context.
- **Shots 2-4.** Each beat is one or two words and runs 1.4-1.65 s. This is slow enough to read and fast enough to feel like speech. The colour temperature cycles (white → saturated green → white) to make three distinct "breaths" without cuts.
- **Shot 5 (wordmark).** A tonal shift from silver to deep green rather than a scale or slide reveal reads as "materialising" or "coming into focus". It is a premium, low-energy reveal that saves energy for the drop.
- **Shot 6 (pill drop).** This is the single high-energy moment of the first half. The white flash (7.967) and pill pop (8.00-8.03) land on a high-frequency transient at 8.03, at the end of the build. The shockwave fills the frame during a ~100 ms pre-drop dropout (8.13-8.23), and the full-band bass hit lands at ≈8.28 s, so the picture leads the drop by ~3 f. Before this come a near-silent gap at ≈6.65-6.80 s, bass re-entering at 7.33 s and a hit at 7.57-7.60 s under the wordmark. The echo rings are a visual sound wave (the pill = the record button, the rings = the voice).
- **Shots 7-8.** This shows the product's value in one before/after. The raw "uh seven no wait seven thirty" is visibly dimmed under the AI-polished line. Streaming speed is the tell: raw words arrive at speech pace (~4.8/s) and polished words at machine pace (~15/s).
- **Shot 9→10.** The icon punches into a B&W world, then colour returns as the voice card appears. This makes a metaphor (kivi brings life and colour) and also handles a jarring style change (vector icon to painterly scene) with an energetic, beat-synced cut.
- **Shot 10 typo fix (20.43).** One micro-moment proves a feature (Custom Dictionary) before it is named in shot 12. The feature is shown first, then labelled, which is good sequencing.
- **Shots 17-18.** The cut-in plus truck plus cursor hover plus click bloom is the only place a "real computer" is simulated. The click becomes the transition, so the transition is motivated by an action.

## 12. Sound Design

- **Loudness:** −10.7 LUFS integrated, which is loud and web-mastered. The level per 0.5 s sits at −12 to −18 dB for most of the film, falls to −49 dB over the last 1.5 s, and drops to about −20 dB around 6.7 s.
- **Music (spectrogram plus onsets):**
  - 0-6.7 s: soft mallet/piano-like plucks with harmonic stacks, plus high "sparkle" blips at 4.4-5 kHz (UI-chime-like). No strong transients, so the onset detector found none before 8.45 s.

  - 8-22 s: rhythmic pulses with the dictation VO on top.
  - **21.7-30.6 s: pad-only ambient section** (horizontal harmonic bands, zero onsets) under the feature title cards.
  - From ≈44 s to the end: steady eighth-note pattern at ≈127 BPM (median inter-onset interval 0.24 s).
  - 76-77.3 s: fade-out.
- **Voice:** there is no narrator. The VO is **in-world user dictation**: curved formant harmonics appear at 9.5-14.5, 18.5-22.5, 40-45, 47-48.5, 54-59.5 and 64.5-67.5 s, exactly where transcript text streams. The speech-likelihood metric (mostly 0.2-0.5, peaks 0.6-0.71 near 40.5-41, 44.5 and 47.5-48 s) under-reports it because the music masks it. Words appear on screen at the same rate as the voice speaks them (≈4-7 words/s).
- **UI SFX:** the high blips in the intro and transients at the cuts and clicks suggest soft interface chimes and ticks [inferred; mixed into the music bed]. There are no obvious whooshes or sub hits.
- **Implications:**
  1. The music carries emotion and the voice carries proof. No VO explains anything, because the demo explains itself.
  2. Silence before the logo plus a riser and drop is a cheap, strong reveal formula.
  3. Dropping to pads under title-card sections makes text-heavy passages feel calm rather than slow.
  4. Rhythmic density rises in the back half to carry the climax.

## 13. Visual Quality

**What makes it premium**
- Ruthless restraint: one sans, one serif, a 3-colour accent family (mint, lime, aqua) and no particles, bounces or 3D gimmicks. Regular-weight type with generous white space.
- Everything breathes. Every hold has a slow push or drift and the aurora is always moving, so there are no dead frames, yet nothing moves fast except the punch and the exits.
- A consistent "say → see" grammar repeated four times. The viewer learns it once and then simply enjoys variations.
- Motivated transitions (a click blooms, the pill becomes the card, the icon punches into its world) and light as the connective tissue.
- Depth through focus: sharp glass UI over strongly defocused painterly plates. Cheap to make and reads as cinematic.
- The colour-bloom metaphor gives emotional lift at each voice start.
- Back-half cuts are locked to the beat grid, mostly within ~1 f.

**Flaws**
- The background plates look AI-illustrated and do not match each other in style: gouache desk, watercolour courtyard, flat-illustration office. The crowd faces in the office (64-69 s) are mushy. The blur hides some of this but not all.
- The B&W→colour device is used on the desk and coder scenes but not the courtyard or office, which is a missed consistency.
- UI micro-text (1.2-2% H) is unreadable at the delivered 1138x640, and the chat card text in shots 7-8 is ≈2% H.
- Artefacts: a 1-frame light-leak patch at 59.73-59.77. The "spreadsheetpowered" overlap at 64.27-64.33 reads as a glitch. The aurora gradients band under compression.
- The four app icons appear with zero stagger and one is an unidentifiable black cube. The icon row is the least crafted moment.
- The CTA is weak: the URL appears alone, with no lockup of logo plus URL and no action verb ("Download", "Try free").
- Mid-film energy dip at 22-31 s: ambient music plus 5 text-only cards in a row.

## 14. Style Category

**Minimal Premium SaaS × Soft-Cinematic UI Demo.** A useful sub-label is **"Airy Aurora / Calm-Tech"**: a light-mode, pastel-gradient, glass-UI, voice/AI product film with in-world dictation audio. ## 15. Transferable Rules (concrete, numeric)

1. **Live-append headline.** Reveal statement lines word by word with a **3-8 f (100-267 ms) stagger**. Centre the line and let it recentre with exponential smoothing: **lerp ≈20-25% per frame, settles in 13-14 f, travel 10-15% W**. Add a linear drift of **≈3-4% W per second** while holding.
2. **Two-tone statements.** Set the subject in near-black (#0B0B0B) and the benefit phrase in a desaturated brand green (#556962 to #396454). Use Regular weight, sentence case, a glyph bbox of **7-10% H (≈70-95 px font at 1080p)** and **≤6 words**, held **1.2-1.7 s**.
3. **Exponential punch into a cut.** For 3-8 frames before a major cut, scale the subject **+18-33% with an expo-in curve** (per-frame increments roughly doubling). Cut on a music onset (within ±1 f) or into a single white frame. Use it at most 4 times in 78 s.
4. **Asymmetric timing.** Entrances run **8-14 f ease-out** and exits **4-6 f ease-in** (a whip or collapse). The entrance-to-exit ratio is about 2:1.
5. **Never fully static.** Every hold gets a **1.07-1.29x push over 0.5-2.5 s** (linear) or the ambient gradient keeps moving.
6. **Voice-card template.** Wide frosted card (**~80-85% W**, top-centre), live waveform card below (**~70% H**), UI text **≈4% H** with leading ≈1.6. Words stream at the VO's pace (**4-7 words/s**), each fading **30%→100% over 3-4 f**. AI-generated text streams at **~2 f per word** to signal machine speed.
7. **Prompt → result split.** Use a continuity hard cut on the same background plate, reframed. Hold the empty result card **~8-9 f (≈280 ms)**, then cascade the content in reading order with a **3-4 f stagger and 4-6 f fades (≈20-22 f total)**. For tables, fill by column with a **2-3 f lag (≈13 f for 5x5)**.
8. **Colour-bloom metaphor.** Start "human world" plates desaturated and bloom to full colour over **22-42 f** as the voice begins. Pair this with the background defocusing.
9. **Light as a transition material.** Use white blooms and washes (**4-7 f** for blooms, **~18 f** for washes) as the default bridge between materials (UI ↔ type card ↔ scene). A click or tap can trigger the bloom.
10. **Reveal formula for the logo.** Near-silence (**~0.15-0.3 s**), a 1 s tonal riser while the wordmark fades from silver to brand colour (**~40 f**, finishing with a left→right tonal sweep), a punch, 1 white frame, and an energetic graphic that fills the frame **~3 f before** the drop hit.
11. **Rhythm.** Keep **type-card shots at 1.2-1.9 s and demo shots at 2.6-6.3 s**. Cut the first half on the narration and the back half on eighth notes (≈127 BPM → **0.236 s grid**). Drop the music to pads under text-heavy stretches.
12. **Feature first, label second.** Show a micro-proof (for example, the auto-corrected name highlight for **~5 f**) before the feature's title card names it.
13. **End structure.** Bookend tagline (aurora returns, same append mechanic, punch) → wordmark **~1.2 s** → symbol crossfade (**9-10 f** each way) → URL **~1.3 s** with a slow push → **~14 f fade to white**, with the music fading from about −15 dB to −49 dB over 1.5 s.

## 16. What to Avoid (as observed here)

- Mismatched illustration styles across lifestyle plates. Lock one rendering style and one light direction for all plates, or use real photography.
- Micro-text below ~2.5% H on a deliverable under 1080p. Either make it big enough to read or make it obviously abstract.
- Transition artefacts (stray previous-frame patches, overlapping words mid-exit). QC every cut at 1-frame stepping.
- Applying a signature device (B&W→colour) to only some of the matching scenes. Use it every time or define the rule.
- Icon rows with zero stagger and an unknown app. Stagger 2-3 f and use recognisable, licensed marks.
- Ending on a bare URL without a logo lockup or a verb-led CTA.
- More than about 4 consecutive text-only cards over an ambient bed. Insert a UI beat or a music lift.
