I made a 32-second credit card launch film with code. No After Effects or Blender, and no AI video generator.

Here's how, in 9 steps 👇

1️⃣ Start from a reference, not a blank page
I picked one film whose look I loved and broke it down frame by frame: how the lines move, when colour arrives, how every cut happens.

2️⃣ Code is the camera
The whole film is React code. Remotion turns it into video; Three.js handles the 3D.

3️⃣ Real 3D models, printed like a banknote
A fox, a balloon, a shell, columns. Every model runs through a custom engraving shader, so it looks printed, not rendered.

4️⃣ Claude Code wrote the code
I directed in plain English ("the fox looks too 3D", "this transition is jerky"). Claude Code wrote the scenes, the shader and the checks.

5️⃣ Storyboard before animation
Every shot was planned first: picture, words, transition and sound. Then real frames were checked before any full render.

6️⃣ 11 rounds of compare-and-fix
Each round put our frames side by side with the reference at the same timestamps. Fix the biggest gap, render, repeat.

7️⃣ Check every frame, not samples
A script measured all 816 frames for pops, stutters and camera lurches. It found jerks that screenshots never show.

8️⃣ Steal the grammar, not the pictures
No dissolves. Hard cuts on the beat, iris opens, a lens sweep, a fish crossing the camera.

9️⃣ The human makes the final cut
I trimmed the intro, swapped one shot and scored it with my own music and sound effects. Code and AI got it most of the way. The edit made it feel finished.

What I learned:
→ A metric said "83% similar" while the film still felt wrong. Your eyes are the judge.
→ Ask for outputs often. Long silent renders hide problems.
→ AI is fast at version 11. Taste picks which version ships.
